## The problem

You get a list `h` of bar heights, each bar 1 unit wide, standing side by side. Rain falls and fills every dip
between the bars until it spills over the lower side. Return how many units of water stay trapped.

For `h = [3, 0, 2, 0, 4, 1, 2]` the answer is `8`. Bars 1 to 3 sit between walls of 3 and 4, so water rises to 3
above them: 3 + 1 + 3 = 7. Bar 5 sits between 4 and 2, so it holds 1 more.

## The key question

> **How high does water rise above one bar?**

Up to the **lower** of two walls: the tallest bar on its left and the tallest bar on its right (each counting the bar
itself). Above that, it would spill over the shorter wall. So the water above bar `i` is
`min(tallest left, tallest right) − h[i]`. Every approach is a faster way to know those two walls.

## Approach 1: Look left and right from every bar

For each bar, scan left for the tallest bar and scan right for the tallest bar, then add the water above it.

<Viz client:visible approach="brute" />

<Complexity time="O(n²)" space="O(1)">
**Time.** Each of the `n` bars scans all `n` bars for its two maximums: **O(n²)**.

**Space.** A few variables: **O(1)**.

**What it wastes:** the tallest bar to the left of `i` and of `i + 1` differ by at most one bar, yet each is found
from scratch.
</Complexity>

## Approach 2: Monotonic stack, filling layer by layer

Instead of working out the water above each bar, fill it in horizontal layers. Keep bars on a stack with heights
falling toward the top. When a taller bar arrives, the bar on top is the **floor** of a dip, the bar now under it is
the **left wall**, and the new bar is the **right wall**. The layer between the walls is `min(left, right) − floor`
deep and `i − left − 1` wide. Pop and repeat while the new bar is still taller. [Monotonic stack
patterns](/concepts/monotonic-stack-patterns/) shows the same "a pop knows both of its walls" idea in the histogram
problem.

<Viz client:visible approach="stack" />

<Complexity time="O(n)" space="O(n)">
**Time.** Each bar is pushed once and popped at most once, and each pop fills one layer in O(1): **O(n)**.

**Space.** If heights only fall, every bar waits on the stack: **O(n)**.

**What it wastes:** the stack itself. Only the two tallest walls matter, not every bar in between.
</Complexity>

## Approach 3: Two pointers (optimal)

Walk `lo` in from the left and `hi` in from the right, tracking the tallest bar seen on each side. If `h[lo] < h[hi]`,
the right side already has a wall at least as tall as `h[lo]`, so the water above `lo` is decided by the left side
alone: `leftMax − h[lo]`. Settle it and move `lo`. Otherwise settle `hi` the same way from the right. This is the same
idea as [Container With Most Water](/dsa/container-with-most-water/): the lower side is the one that can't do better.

<Viz client:visible approach="twoPointers" />

<Complexity time="O(n)" space="O(1)">
**Time.** Each step settles one bar and moves one pointer inward: **O(n)**.

**Space.** Four numbers: **O(1)**.
</Complexity>

Race two approaches on the same input. Both share one clock that ticks once per operation, so the one doing extra work visibly falls behind.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the taller wall.** Water spills over the *lower* of the two walls, so take the `min`.
- **Popping the last bar and still adding water.** With nothing left under the floor there's no left wall, and the
  water would spill out: stop.
- **Moving the taller pointer.** In the two-pointer walk, settle and move the *lower* side; the taller side's water
  still depends on bars not seen yet.
