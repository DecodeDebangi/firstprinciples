## The problem

You get the root of a binary tree. Imagine standing to its right: return the values you can see, top to bottom. That's the rightmost node of each level.

For the tree `[5, 3, 8, 1, 4, null, 9, null, 2]` the answer is `[5, 8, 9, 2]`. The 2 is a left child, but nothing on its level is further right.

## The key question

> **What makes a node visible from the right?**

Being the last node on its level. So the answer is one value per level, and a [level-by-level walk](/concepts/tree-traversals/) hands you each level in order.

## Approach 1: One walk down per level

For each depth, walk down from the root, trying the right child before the left, and stop at the first node found at that depth.

<Viz client:visible approach="rewalk" />

<Complexity time="O(n · h)" space="O(h)">
**Time.** Each depth re-walks every level above it: **O(n · h)**, O(n²) for a chain.

**Space.** The recursion: **O(h)**.

**What it wastes:** the upper levels are walked again for every level below them.
</Complexity>

## Approach 2: One breadth-first walk, a level at a time (optimal)

Use a queue. At the start of each level its length is the level's size; take that many nodes, queueing their children, and record the last one.

<Viz client:visible approach="bfs" />

<Complexity time="O(n)" space="O(w)">
**Time.** Each node is queued and taken once: **O(n)**.

**Space.** The queue holds at most one level: **O(w)**, the tree's widest level.
</Complexity>

## Comparing the approaches

Race two approaches on the same input. Both share one clock that ticks once per operation, so the one doing extra work visibly falls behind.

<Compare client:visible />

| Approach | Time | Space | Idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| One walk down per level | O(n · h) | O(h) | Restart from the root per level |
| One breadth-first walk, a level at a time | **O(n)** | O(w) | The last node of each level |

## Common mistakes

- **Only following right children.** A left child is visible when nothing on its level is to its right.
- **Recording the first node of each level.** That's the view from the left.
- **Losing track of where a level ends.** Read the queue's length before taking the level.
