## The problem

A row of houses each holds some money, given as a list of whole numbers. Robbing two **neighbouring** houses sets
off an alarm. Return the most money you can take without robbing any two neighbours.

For `[3, 8, 2, 4, 7, 1]` the answer is **15**: rob the houses holding 8 and 7 (positions 1 and 4).

## The key question

> **For each house there are only two choices: rob it or skip it. But the best choice depends on the houses after
> it. How do I avoid working out the same "best from here on" over and over?**

## Approach 1: Plain recursion, rob it or skip it

Let `best(i)` be the most you can take from houses `i` to the end. House `i` is either skipped, giving `best(i + 1)`,
or robbed, giving its money plus `best(i + 2)` (the next house must be skipped). Try both and keep the bigger.

<Viz client:visible approach="recurse" />

<Complexity time="O(1.6ⁿ)" space="O(n)">
**Time.** Each call makes two more, one for i + 1 and one for i + 2, so the number of calls grows like the Fibonacci
numbers: roughly **1.6ⁿ**. At n = 40 that's hundreds of millions of calls.

**Space.** The recursion goes up to n calls deep: **O(n)**.

**What it wastes:** there are only n different questions, `best(0)` to `best(n − 1)`, but each is answered again
every time it comes up.
</Complexity>

## Approach 2: Fill a table left to right

Turn it around: let `dp[i]` be the most from houses 0 to `i`. House `i` is either skipped, keeping `dp[i − 1]`, or
robbed, adding its money to `dp[i − 2]`. Each entry needs only the two before it, so fill the table from the left.
This is the take-or-skip shape from [Designing a 1D DP](/concepts/one-dimensional-dp/).

<Viz client:visible approach="table" />

<Complexity time="O(n)" space="O(1)">
**Time.** One pass, O(1) work per house: **O(n)**.

**Space.** The table as drawn is O(n), but each entry only reads the two before it, so two variables are enough:
**O(1)**.

**What it wastes:** nothing. Every house must be looked at once.
</Complexity>

## Comparing the approaches

Step through both side by side. Both share one clock that ticks once per operation.

| Approach | Time | Extra space | Idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plain recursion | O(1.6ⁿ) | O(n) | Try robbing and skipping every house |
| Table | **O(n)** | **O(1)** | dp[i] = max(dp[i − 1], dp[i − 2] + nums[i]) |

Adding memoisation to the recursion (saving each `best(i)` the first time) also gives O(n), with O(n) space.

## Common mistakes

- **Robbing every other house.** Alternating isn't always best: in `[2, 1, 1, 9]`, rob the first and last for 11.
- **Forgetting one house or two.** With one house, rob it; with two, take the bigger. Handle these before the loop.
- **Using `dp[i − 1] + nums[i]`.** That robs two neighbours; taking house i builds on `dp[i − 2]`.
- **Keeping a greedy "take the biggest first".** The biggest house can block two neighbours worth more together.
