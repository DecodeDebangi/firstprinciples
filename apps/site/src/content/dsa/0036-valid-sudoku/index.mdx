## The problem

You get a 9 × 9 board. Some cells hold a digit from 1 to 9; the rest are empty, written `.`. Decide whether the
digits so far break the rules: **no digit may appear twice in the same row, the same column, or the same 3 × 3 box**
(the board is split into nine boxes of 3 × 3 cells). You don't have to check whether the puzzle can be solved, only
the digits already there.

For example, a board with a 5 at row 6, column 8 and another 5 at row 8, column 7 is invalid. They're in different
rows and different columns, but both are in the bottom-right box.

## The key question

> **How do I know which digits are already in a cell's row, column and box, without searching them every time?**

Rows and columns are easy to name. Boxes need a number too: as in [Grids](/concepts/grids/), the cell `(r, c)` is in
box `floor(r / 3) · 3 + floor(c / 3)`, numbered 0 to 8 in reading order.

Below, `n` is the side of the board (9 here). Because the board is always 9 × 9, every approach is technically
O(1); the bounds in terms of `n` show how the work would grow on a bigger board.

## Approach 1: Scan each digit's row, column and box

For each digit, look at the other 8 cells of its row, the other 8 of its column and the other 8 of its box. The box
starts at row `r - r % 3` and column `c - c % 3`: rounding down to a multiple of 3 finds its top left corner.

<Viz client:visible approach="scan" />

<Complexity time="O(n³)" space="O(1)">
**Time.** Up to `n²` digits, each compared with about `3n` other cells: **O(n³)**. On a 9 × 9 board that's at most
81 · 24 = 1,944 comparisons.

**Space.** A few indices: **O(1)**.

**What it wastes:** the same row is searched again for every digit in it. A set per row, column and box would remember
what's been seen and answer in one lookup.
</Complexity>

## Approach 2: One pass with a set per row, column and box (optimal)

Keep 27 [hash sets](/concepts/how-hash-maps-work/): `rows[r]`, `cols[c]` and `boxes[b]` hold the digits seen so far
in each. For each digit, check its three sets; if it's in any of them, that's a repeat. Otherwise add it to all three.
It's the "have I seen it?" move from [Hashing patterns](/concepts/hashing-patterns/), three times over.

<Viz client:visible approach="sets" />

<Complexity time="O(n²)" space="O(n²)">
**Time.** One visit per cell, with three O(1) lookups and three additions for each digit: **O(n²)**, at most 81 · 6
set operations on a 9 × 9 board.

**Space.** `3n` sets, each holding up to `n` digits: **O(n²)**, at most 243 digits.
</Complexity>

Because each digit is between 1 and 9, each set can also be a list of 9 true/false values, one per digit. The idea is
the same.

## Comparing the approaches

Both on the same board:

<Compare client:visible />

| Approach | Time | Extra space | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scan row, column and box | O(n³) | O(1) | up to 24 comparisons per digit |
| A set per row, column and box | O(n²) | O(n²) | three lookups per digit |

The board never grows past 9 × 9, so there's no growth chart: the difference is about 24 comparisons per digit
against 3 lookups.

## Common mistakes

- **The wrong box number.** `floor(r / 3) + floor(c / 3)` gives the same number to different boxes: the box right of
  the top-left one and the box below it would both be 1. Multiplying one part by 3 keeps all nine apart.
- **Counting empty cells.** Skip `.` before checking; otherwise every empty cell looks like a repeat of the first.
- **Checking whether the puzzle can be solved.** The problem only asks whether the digits already there break a rule.
