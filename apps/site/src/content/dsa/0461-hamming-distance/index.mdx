## The problem

You get two non-negative whole numbers `x` and `y`. Return their **Hamming distance**: the number of bit positions
where one has a 0 and the other has a 1.

For example, `37 = 100101` and `22 = 010110` differ in the places worth 32, 16, 2 and 1, so the distance is 4.

## The key question

> **Is there one operation that marks every place where two numbers differ?**

See [Binary](/concepts/binary-and-bits/) for XOR, and [Bit tricks](/concepts/bit-tricks/) for counting 1s quickly.

## Approach 1: Compare the last bits, shift, repeat

Compare `x & 1` with `y & 1`, count a difference, then shift both right. Stop when both are 0.

<Viz client:visible approach="bitByBit" />

<Complexity time="O(b)" space="O(1)">
**Time.** One loop per bit up to the larger number's highest 1. With 32-bit numbers that's at most 32 loops: **O(b)**
for `b` bits, which is O(1) for a fixed size.

**Space.** A few numbers: **O(1)**.

**What it wastes:** a separate comparison for every place, including all the places that match. XOR compares every
place at once.
</Complexity>

## Approach 2: XOR, then count the 1s (optimal)

`z = x xor y` has a 1 exactly where `x` and `y` differ. So the distance is the number of 1s in `z`, which is
[Number of 1 Bits](/dsa/number-of-1-bits/): remove the lowest 1 with `z & (z − 1)` until nothing is left.

<Viz client:visible approach="xorCount" />

<Complexity time="O(1)" space="O(1)">
**Time.** One XOR, then one loop per difference, at most 32: **O(1)** for fixed-size numbers, and never more loops
than approach 1.

**Space.** **O(1)**.
</Complexity>

## Comparing the approaches

Both on the same numbers:

<Compare client:visible />

| Approach | Time | Extra space | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compare bit by bit | O(b) | O(1) | one loop per bit |
| XOR, then count | O(k), k differences | O(1) | one loop per difference |

Both are at most 32 loops for 32-bit numbers, so there's no growth chart.

## Common mistakes

- **Stopping when only one number reaches 0.** The other may still have 1s; those places differ from the 0s of the
  shorter one.
- **Comparing decimal digits.** The distance is about bits: 1 and 2 differ in two places (`01` and `10`).
