## The problem

You get two words `a` and `b`. A **subsequence** of a word keeps some of its letters in their original order, skipping
any others: `cat` is a subsequence of `chart`. Return the length of the longest subsequence the two words have in
common, or 0 if they share no letters.

For example, `cart` and `chat` have the common subsequence `cat`, so the answer is 3.

Below, `m` and `n` are the lengths of `a` and `b`.

## The key question

> **How can the answer for two words be built from answers for shorter beginnings of them?**

See [DP on two strings](/concepts/dp-on-two-strings/), which builds this exact table step by step.

## Approach 1: Try every subsequence of a

Every letter of `a` is either kept or skipped, so `a` has `2^m` subsequences. Check each against `b` and keep the longest
that fits.

<Viz client:visible approach="everySub" />

<Complexity time="O(2^m · n)" space="O(m)">
**Time.** `2^m` subsequences, each checked against `b` in O(n): **O(2^m · n)**.

**Space.** One subsequence at a time: **O(m)**.

**What it wastes:** subsequences that share a beginning are checked from scratch every time. The answers depend only on
how much of each word has been used, which is just `(m + 1)(n + 1)` different situations.
</Complexity>

## Approach 2: Fill a table (optimal)

`dp[i][j]` is the answer for the first `i` letters of `a` and the first `j` of `b`. Row 0 and column 0 are 0 (no
letters). If the last letters match, `dp[i][j] = dp[i − 1][j − 1] + 1`; otherwise `dp[i][j]` is the larger of the cell
above and the cell to the left.

<Viz client:visible approach="table" />

<Complexity time="O(m · n)" space="O(m · n)">
**Time.** One constant-time rule per cell: **O(m · n)**.

**Space.** The table, **O(m · n)**. Each row only reads the row above, so two rows are enough for the length:
**O(min(m, n))**, by putting the shorter word along the top.
</Complexity>

## Common mistakes

- **Confusing subsequence with substring.** The common letters needn't be next to each other in either word.
- **Taking dp[i − 1][j − 1] when the letters differ.** Then both letters are dropped, which can lose a match: use the
  better of above and left.
- **Off-by-one on the letters.** Row `i` stands for the first `i` letters, so its last letter is `a[i − 1]`.
