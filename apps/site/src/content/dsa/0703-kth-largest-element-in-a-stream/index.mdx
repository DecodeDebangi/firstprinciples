## The problem

Design a class that's given a number `k` and some starting numbers. Then numbers arrive one at a time through
`add(val)`, and after each one, `add` returns the **kth largest** number seen so far (counting duplicates).

With `k = 3` and starting numbers `[5, 1, 8, 3]`, adding `6, 2, 9, 4` returns `5, 5, 6, 6`. After adding 6, the
numbers are 1, 3, 5, 6, 8, and the third largest is 5.

## The key question

> **To know the kth largest, which numbers do I actually need to remember?**

Only the **k largest** so far. Anything smaller than all of them can never become the kth largest, however many
numbers arrive later. And of those k, the answer is the **smallest**: exactly what a min-heap keeps on top.

## Approach 1: Keep everything sorted

Store every number in a sorted list. Binary search finds where a new number goes, then everything after it shifts
right one place. The kth largest is `k` places from the end.

<Viz client:visible approach="sorted" />

<Complexity time="O(n) per add" space="O(n)">
**Time.** The binary search is O(log n), but inserting into the middle of a list shifts every larger value: **O(n)**
per add, where `n` is how many numbers have arrived so far.

**Space.** Every number ever added is kept: **O(n)**, growing forever on an endless stream.

**What it wastes:** it keeps, and keeps shifting, numbers far too small to ever be the answer.
</Complexity>

## Approach 2: Min-heap of the k largest (optimal)

Keep at most `k` numbers in a **min-heap**. Push each new number; if that makes `k + 1`, pop the smallest. The
top of the heap is then the smallest of the k largest: the kth largest.

It feels backwards at first: we want the *largest* numbers, so why a *min*-heap? Because the number we need to
look at, and the one we need to throw away, are both the **smallest of the ones we keep**.

<Viz client:visible approach="heap" />

<Complexity time="O(log k) per add" space="O(k)">
**Time.** Each add is one push and at most one pop on a heap of at most `k + 1` items: **O(log k)**. Reading the
top is O(1). Building from `n` starting numbers costs O(n log k).

**Space.** Never more than `k + 1` numbers, however long the stream: **O(k)**.
</Complexity>

## Comparing the approaches

Race the two on the same input. Both share one clock that ticks once per operation. With only a few numbers they're close; the gap is in how the cost **grows**: the sorted list shifts more with every number it stores, while the heap never holds more than k + 1. The chart below shows it.

| Approach | Each add | Space | Idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keep everything sorted | O(n) | O(n) | Insert in order, read k from the end |
| Min-heap of size k | **O(log k)** | **O(k)** | Keep only the k largest; the top is the answer |

## Common mistakes

- **Using a max-heap.** Then the kth largest is buried k levels down. A min-heap of size k puts it on top.
- **Popping before pushing.** Push the new number first, then pop if the heap has `k + 1`. Popping first can throw
  away a number that should stay.
- **Building the heap with all starting numbers and never trimming it.** Trim to `k` (or route the starting numbers
  through `add`) or the top isn't the kth largest.
- **`>= k` instead of `> k`.** Pop only when the heap holds more than `k`. Popping at `k` leaves `k − 1` numbers, and
  the top becomes the (k − 1)th largest.
