## The problem

A researcher has `n` papers, and `citations[i]` is how many times paper `i` has been cited. Their **h-index** is the
largest number `h` such that at least `h` of their papers have **at least** `h` citations each.

For `[4, 0, 6, 1, 5, 3]` the h-index is **3**: four papers have at least 3 citations (4, 6, 5 and 3), but only three
have at least 4.

## The key question

> **h can't be more than n, the number of papers. For a given h, how quickly can I count the papers with at least h
> citations?**

## Approach 1: Try each h, counting every time

Try h = n, then n − 1, and so on. For each one, scan every paper and count those with at least h citations. The first
h that has enough is the answer.

<Viz client:visible approach="tryEach" />

<Complexity time="O(n²)" space="O(1)">
**Time.** Up to n + 1 guesses, each scanning n papers: **O(n²)**.

**Space.** One counter: **O(1)**.

**What it wastes:** each guess recounts from scratch, though the papers with at least h citations are exactly those
with at least h + 1, plus the ones with exactly h.
</Complexity>

## Approach 2: Sort from most cited, then scan

Sort the citations from largest to smallest. The paper at index `i` is then the (i + 1)th most cited, so if it has more
than `i` citations, the top i + 1 papers all have at least i + 1. Walk forward while that holds.

<Viz client:visible approach="sort" />

<Complexity time="O(n log n)" space="O(1) to O(n)">
**Time.** The sort is **O(n log n)**; the scan is O(n).

**Space.** Depends on the sort: **O(1)** in place, up to **O(n)** for many built-in sorts.

**What it wastes:** it fully orders papers with 500 and 600 citations, when, for a researcher with 10 papers, any
count of 10 or more counts the same.
</Complexity>

## Approach 3: Count papers per citation level

Since h is at most n, a paper with more than n citations helps exactly as much as one with n. So make n + 1 buckets,
count the papers at each level (anything above n goes in bucket n), then walk down from h = n, adding each bucket to
a running total of papers with **at least** h citations. Stop at the first h where the total reaches h. This is the
counting idea from [Sorting as a tool](/concepts/sorting-as-a-tool/).

<Viz client:visible approach="count" />

<Complexity time="O(n)" space="O(n)">
**Time.** One pass to fill the buckets and at most one pass down them: **O(n)**.

**Space.** n + 1 buckets: **O(n)**.

**What it wastes:** extra memory, compared with sorting in place. It's the fastest possible, since every paper
must be looked at once.
</Complexity>

## Comparing the approaches

Step through all three side by side. They share one clock that ticks once per operation.

<Compare client:visible />

| Approach | Time | Extra space | Idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| Try each h | O(n²) | O(1) | Recount for every guess |
| Sort, then scan | O(n log n) | O(1) to O(n) | Index i has the (i + 1)th most citations |
| Count per level | **O(n)** | O(n) | Cap at n, total from the top |

## Common mistakes

- **Returning a citation count instead of a paper count.** For `[100]` the answer is 1, not 100.
- **Making a bucket per citation value.** Counts can be huge; cap them at n.
- **Using ≥ instead of > in the sorted scan.** With 0-based indexes, index i needs **more than** i citations for
  h to reach i + 1.
- **Forgetting papers with zero citations.** For `[0, 0]` the answer is 0.
