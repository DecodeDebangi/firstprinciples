## The problem

You get a list `prices`, one per item in a shop, in order. Each item gets a discount equal to the price of the
**first item after it** whose price is **no higher** than its own. If there's no such item, it gets no discount.
Return what you actually pay for each item.

For `prices = [9, 4, 6, 2, 7, 7, 3]` you pay `[5, 2, 4, 2, 0, 4, 3]`. The 9 is discounted by the 4 right after it.
The first 7 is discounted by the equal 7 after it, so it's free. The 2 and the last 3 have nothing cheaper after
them, so they pay in full.

## The key question

> **When a cheap item arrives, which earlier items does it discount?**

Every earlier item that's still waiting and costs at least as much, and those are the most recent ones still
waiting. "Most recent first" is a stack, the same one as [Daily Temperatures](/dsa/daily-temperatures/), looking
for "no higher" instead of "warmer".

## Approach 1: Look right from each item

For each item, scan to the right until a price no higher than its own appears.

<Viz client:visible approach="scan" />

<Complexity time="O(n²)" space="O(1) extra">
**Time.** If prices only rise, no scan finds a discount, so item `i` checks every later item: `n(n − 1)/2` checks,
**O(n²)**.

**Space.** Nothing beyond the answer: **O(1) extra**.

**What it wastes:** a run of rising prices is scanned again by every item before it.
</Complexity>

## Approach 2: Monotonic stack (optimal)

Walk once, keeping items still waiting for their discount on a stack. A new price pops every waiting item that costs
**at least as much** (`≥`) and gives it the discount, then waits itself. The prices on the stack rise toward the top.
[Monotonic stack patterns](/concepts/monotonic-stack-patterns/) shows how the pop test picks which question a stack
answers.

<Viz client:visible approach="stack" />

<Complexity time="O(n)" space="O(n)">
**Time.** Every item is pushed once and popped at most once, so all the `while` loops together run at most `n` times:
**O(n)**.

**Space.** If prices only rise, every item waits on the stack: **O(n)**.
</Complexity>

## Comparing the approaches

Race two approaches on the same input. Both share one clock that ticks once per operation, so the one doing extra work visibly falls behind.

| Approach | Time | Space | Idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| Look right from each item | O(n²) | O(1) | Each item scans for its own discount |

## Common mistakes

- **Popping only strictly higher prices.** An equal price counts as a discount, so pop while the top is `≥` the new
  price, not `>`.
- **Discounting by the cheapest later price.** It's the *first* one that's no higher, not the lowest anywhere after.
- **Changing `prices` while reading it.** Write the results into a copy, or later items read discounted prices.
