## The problem

Design a class with two operations: `addNum(x)` takes in one more whole number, and `findMedian()` returns the
**median** of every number added so far. The median is the middle value once the numbers are sorted; with an even
count it's the average of the two middle values.

Adding 6, 3, 9, 4, 1 and 7 one by one, the medians after each add are 6, 4.5, 6, 5, 4 and 5.

## The key question

> **The median only needs the one or two values in the middle of the sorted order. Can I keep just the middle
> handy, without keeping everything in order?**

## Approach 1: Keep every number sorted

Store the numbers in a list kept sorted. Binary search finds where each new number goes, and the median is the middle
of the list.

<Viz client:visible approach="sorted" />

<Complexity time="O(n) per add, O(1) per median" space="O(n)">
**Time.** Binary search finds the spot in O(log n), but inserting into a list shifts every larger value one place:
**O(n)** per add, so O(n²) for a stream of n. Reading the median is **O(1)**.

**Space.** Every number is stored: **O(n)**.

**What it wastes:** it keeps the whole list in order, when the median only ever looks at the middle.
</Complexity>

## Approach 2: Two heaps, the smaller and larger halves

Split the numbers into two halves. A **max-heap** holds the smaller half, so its top is the biggest of the small
numbers; a **min-heap** holds the larger half, so its top is the smallest of the big numbers. Those two tops sit
right at the middle of the sorted order. The third pattern in [Heaps in practice](/concepts/heaps-in-practice/).

To add a number, compare it with the smaller half's top to pick its half. Then, if the sizes drift more than one
apart (the smaller half may hold one extra), move one top across.

<Viz client:visible approach="heaps" />

<Complexity time="O(log n) per add, O(1) per median" space="O(n)">
**Time.** An add is one push plus at most one pop and one push to rebalance, each **O(log n)**. The median reads
one or two tops: **O(1)**.

**Space.** Every number sits in one of the heaps: **O(n)**.

**What it wastes:** nothing for a general stream. If every number is known to be small (say 0 to 100), a count per
value gives O(1) adds and O(100) medians.
</Complexity>

## Common mistakes

- **Sorting on every `findMedian()` call.** That's O(n log n) per call.
- **Putting the new number in the smaller heap no matter what.** Every number in the smaller half must be at most
  every number in the larger half; compare with the top first.
- **Letting the halves drift apart.** After each add, the sizes must be equal or the smaller half one ahead.
- **Integer division for the even case.** `(4 + 5) / 2` must give 4.5, not 4.
