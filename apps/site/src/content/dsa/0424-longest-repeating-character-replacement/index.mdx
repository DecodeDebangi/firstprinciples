## The problem

You get a string `s` of capital letters and a number `k`. You may change any letter to any other letter, at most `k`
times in total. Return the length of the longest stretch you can make in which every letter is the same.

For example, `BAABCBBA` with `k = 2`: the stretch `BCBBA` (positions 3 to 7) becomes `BBBBB` by changing the C and
the A, so length 5 is possible. Every stretch of 6 would need at least 3 changes, so the answer is 5.

## The key question

> **How many changes does a window need to become a single repeated letter?**

Keep its most common letter and change everything else: `length − (count of the most common letter)`. So this is the
"longest window within a budget" from [Window budgets](/concepts/window-budgets/), with that as the cost.

## Approach 1: From every start, extend while it can be fixed

For each start, extend to the right, keeping letter counts and the biggest count, until the cost goes over `k`.

<Viz client:visible approach="everyStart" />

<Complexity time="O(n²)" space="O(1)">
**Time.** Each start can scan to the end: **O(n²)**.

**Space.** At most 26 letter counts: **O(1)**.

**What it wastes:** the next start's window overlaps this one, and all its letters are counted again.
</Complexity>

## Approach 2: One window with letter counts (optimal)

Grow the window one letter at a time, updating its count. While its cost (length minus the largest count, `max(counts)`)
is more than `k`, drop letters from the left. After each step, the window is fixable, so compare its length with the
best.

<Viz client:visible approach="window" />

<Complexity time="O(n)" space="O(1)">
**Time.** Each letter enters and leaves once, and the largest of 26 counts is found in 26 steps: O(26 · n) = **O(n)**.

**Space.** 26 counts: **O(1)**.
</Complexity>

A well-known refinement keeps a running "largest count ever seen" instead of recomputing the maximum. It never shrinks
the window below its best length, which is all the answer needs, and drops the factor of 26.

## Common mistakes

- **Counting the letter to keep as the first letter of the window.** Keep the most common one, which can change as the
  window moves.
- **Shrinking with `if` instead of `while`.** Dropping one letter doesn't always lower the cost: if it was one of the
  most common letters, the length and the largest count both go down by one. Keep shrinking until the window is
  fixable.
- **Forgetting that letters outside the window don't matter.** Only the window's own counts decide its cost.
