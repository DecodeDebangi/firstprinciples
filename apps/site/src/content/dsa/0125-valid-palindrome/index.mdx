## The problem

You get a string `s`. Keep only its letters and digits, lower-case the letters, and check whether what's left
reads the same forwards and backwards. Return `True` or `False`.

`"Top spot!"` cleans to `"topspot"`, which is a palindrome. `"race a car"` cleans to `"raceacar"`, which isn't.

## The key question

> **Do I need a cleaned copy at all, or can I compare the original string from both ends?**

A palindrome check only ever compares the first unchecked character with the last unchecked one. Both
approaches below do that comparison; the second does it without making any copies.

## Approach 1: Clean, reverse, compare

Build the cleaned string, build its reverse, and compare them position by position. It's short to write, but it
makes two new strings and compares every pair twice: position 1 with position n, and later position n with
position 1.

<Viz client:visible approach="reverse" />

<Complexity time="O(n)" space="O(n)">
**Time.** Cleaning reads `n` characters, reversing reads them again, and the comparison reads them a third
time: about `3n` steps, **O(n)**.

**Space.** The cleaned copy and the reversed copy each hold up to `n` characters: **O(n)**.

**What it wastes:** two copies of the string, and a second pass over pairs it has already compared.
</Complexity>

## Approach 2: Two pointers, in place (optimal)

Put `i` at the start and `j` at the end. If either sits on a space or a punctuation mark, step past it. Otherwise
compare the two characters, ignoring case: a mismatch means `False`, a match moves both inward. When the
pointers meet, every pair has matched.

<Viz client:visible approach="pointers" />

<Complexity time="O(n)" space="O(1)">
**Time.** Every step moves `i` right or `j` left (or both), and they stop when they meet, so there are at most
`n` steps, each O(1): **O(n)**. On a clean palindrome that's only `n/2` comparisons.

**Space.** Two indices, whatever the length of `s`: **O(1)**.
</Complexity>

## Comparing the approaches

Race two approaches on the same input. Both share one clock that ticks once per operation, so the one doing extra work visibly falls behind. Choose any pair, or type your own input.

| Approach | Time | Space | Idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clean, reverse, compare | O(n) | O(n) | Make copies, then compare everything |
| Two pointers | **O(n)** | **O(1)** | Compare from both ends, skipping as you go |

Both are O(n); the difference is the constant and the memory. The chart counts work on a palindrome of `a`s, where
nothing stops early.

## Common mistakes

- **Forgetting digits.** `"0P"` is not a palindrome: `0` counts as a character.
- **Comparing before skipping.** Skip non-alphanumeric characters first, then compare, or `"a."` fails.
- **Case.** Compare `s[i].lower()` with `s[j].lower()`; `"Aa"` is a palindrome.
- **Running past each other.** The loop condition is `i < j`; skipping can push one pointer past the other.
