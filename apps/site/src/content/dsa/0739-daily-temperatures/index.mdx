## The problem

You get a list `temps` of daily temperatures. Return a list `answer` where `answer[i]` is the number of days
after day `i` until a **warmer** temperature. If no warmer day comes, `answer[i]` is `0`.

For `temps = [70, 74, 71, 69, 72, 76, 73]` the answer is `[1, 4, 2, 1, 1, 0, 0]`.

## The key question

> **Instead of each day looking forward, can each new day look back and settle the days still waiting?**

When a warm day arrives, it answers every earlier, cooler day that hasn't found its warmer day yet, and those are
always the most recent ones. "Most recent first" is a stack.

## Approach 1: Brute force, look ahead from each day

For each day, scan forward until a warmer one appears.

<Viz client:visible approach="brute" />

<Complexity time="O(n²)" space="O(1) extra">
**Time.** When temperatures only fall, every scan runs to the end: `n(n − 1)/2` comparisons, **O(n²)**.

**Space.** Nothing beyond the answer: **O(1) extra**.

**What it wastes:** the same stretch of cool days is re-scanned by every day before it.
</Complexity>

## Approach 2: Monotonic stack (optimal)

Walk forward once with a stack of days still waiting for a warmer day. Each new day pops every waiting day that's
cooler, recording how long each waited, then pushes itself. Reading from bottom to top, the temperatures on the stack
never rise, which is why it's called a **monotonic** stack.

<Viz client:visible approach="stack" />

<Complexity time="O(n)" space="O(n)">
**Time.** Every day is pushed once and popped at most once, so all the `while` loops together run at most `n`
times: **O(n)** overall, even though one day can pop several.

**Space.** If temperatures only fall, every day waits on the stack: **O(n)**.
</Complexity>

## Common mistakes

- **Storing temperatures instead of indices.** The answer is a distance, so the stack must hold days (indices).
- **`<=` instead of `<`.** Only a strictly warmer day counts; an equal temperature keeps waiting.
- **Thinking the nested loop makes it O(n²).** Count pushes and pops instead: each happens at most once per day.
