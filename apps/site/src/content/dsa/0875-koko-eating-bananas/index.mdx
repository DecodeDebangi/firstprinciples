## The problem

There are `n` piles of bananas and `h` hours. Each hour, Koko picks one pile and eats up to `k` bananas from it; if the
pile has fewer, she finishes it and waits for the next hour. Return the smallest whole speed `k` that lets her finish
every pile within `h` hours. (`h` is at least the number of piles, so it's always possible.)

At speed `k`, a pile of `p` takes `⌈p / k⌉` hours: divide and round up. For example, with piles `5, 9, 2, 6` and
`h = 7`, speed 4 needs 2 + 3 + 1 + 2 = 8 hours, too many, but speed 5 needs 1 + 2 + 1 + 2 = 6. The answer is 5.

Below, `m` is the biggest pile.

## The key question

> **There's no list to search. What range of answers is possible, and how do I test one?**

The speed is between 1 and `m` (eating faster than the biggest pile never helps), and testing a speed means adding up
the hours. See [Binary search beyond sorted arrays](/concepts/binary-search-on-answers/).

## Approach 1: Try speeds 1, 2, 3, …

Test each speed in turn and stop at the first that's fast enough.

<Viz client:visible approach="linear" />

<Complexity time="O(n · m)" space="O(1)">
**Time.** Up to `m` speeds, each tested in O(n): **O(n · m)**. Piles can hold a billion bananas.

**Space.** **O(1)**.

**What it wastes:** a "too slow" answer at speed `k` also means every slower speed is too slow, and a "fast enough"
means every faster speed works. Testing one speed at a time ignores that.
</Complexity>

## Approach 2: Binary search the speeds (optimal)

Search speeds 1 to `m` with the first-yes template: if the middle speed is fast enough, the answer is there or slower;
if not, it's faster.

<Viz client:visible approach="binary" />

<Complexity time="O(n log m)" space="O(1)">
**Time.** About `log₂ m` tests, each O(n): **O(n log m)**. For a billion bananas, around 30 tests.

**Space.** **O(1)**.
</Complexity>

## Common mistakes

- **Rounding the hours down.** A pile of 7 at speed 2 takes 4 hours, not 3: `⌈7 / 2⌉`.
- **Starting the search at 0.** A speed of 0 never finishes; start at 1.
- **Overflowing the hour count.** With many huge piles at speed 1, the total can pass a 32-bit integer; use a 64-bit
  count, or stop adding once it passes `h`.
