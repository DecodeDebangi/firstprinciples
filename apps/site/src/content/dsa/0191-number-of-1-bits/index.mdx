## The problem

You get a positive whole number `n`. Return how many 1s there are in its binary form (this is also called its
**Hamming weight**).

For example, `104` is `1101000` in binary, which has three 1s, so the answer is 3. The animations show 8 bits; the real
problem allows up to 32.

## The key question

> **Can I visit only the 1 bits, and skip the 0s?**

See [Binary](/concepts/binary-and-bits/) for reading bits with `&` and `>>`, and [Bit tricks](/concepts/bit-tricks/)
for removing the lowest 1.

## Approach 1: Check the last bit, shift, repeat

`n & 1` is the last bit. Add it to the count, then shift `n` right by one so the next bit takes its place. Stop when
`n` is 0.

<Viz client:visible approach="shift" />

<Complexity time="O(b)" space="O(1)">
**Time.** One loop per bit up to the highest 1. With `b` bits that's **O(b)**, at most 32.

**Space.** Two numbers: **O(1)**.

**What it wastes:** a loop for every 0 bit, which adds nothing to the count. A number like `10000000…` has one 1 but
needs the full walk.
</Complexity>

## Approach 2: Drop the lowest 1, repeat (optimal)

`n & (n − 1)` removes the lowest 1 bit of `n` and leaves the rest alone. Do it until `n` is 0, counting the steps.

<Viz client:visible approach="drop" />

<Complexity time="O(k)" space="O(1)">
**Time.** One loop per 1 bit. With `k` ones that's **O(k)**, never more than approach 1.

**Space.** Two numbers: **O(1)**.
</Complexity>

Many languages and processors also have a built-in "population count" that does it in one instruction.

## Common mistakes

- **Using a signed shift on a negative number.** In Java, `>>` copies the sign bit in from the left, so a negative
  number never reaches 0. Use `>>>`, or the `n & (n − 1)` loop, which stops either way.
- **Counting with `n % 10`.** That reads decimal digits, not bits.
