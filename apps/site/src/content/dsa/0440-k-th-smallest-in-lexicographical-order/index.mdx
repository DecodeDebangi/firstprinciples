## The problem

Given n and k, return the k-th number when 1 to n are listed in dictionary order (compared as text, so 10 comes before 2). n can be as large as a billion.

For `n = 23, k = 9` the order begins 1, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, so the answer is 17.

## The key question

> **Can you jump over many numbers at once without listing them?**

Yes. The numbers starting with p sit together in the order, and how many there are can be counted directly: those between p and p, then between 10p and 10p + 9, then 100p to 100p + 99, and so on, each capped at n.

## Approach 1: Walk the order one number at a time

Run the walk from Lexicographical Numbers and stop after k numbers.

<Viz client:visible approach="walk" />

<Complexity time="O(k)" space="O(1)">
**Time.** k steps: **O(k)**, which can be close to n, a billion.

**Space.** Only cur: **O(1)**.

**What it wastes:** visiting every number before the k-th, when whole blocks of them could be skipped at once.
</Complexity>

## Approach 2: Count whole subtrees and skip them (optimal)

Start at 1, with k − 1 more steps to take. Count the numbers starting with cur. If they're no more than the steps left, skip them all: move to cur + 1. Otherwise the answer is inside: move to cur × 10, using one step for cur itself.

<Viz client:visible approach="skip" />

<Complexity time="O(D²)" space="O(1)">
**Time.** At most D levels, with up to 10 moves sideways on each, and every count takes D steps: about **O(D²)** with a constant of 10, about a thousand steps for a billion.

**Space.** A few numbers: **O(1)**.
</Complexity>

## Comparing the approaches

Race two approaches on the same input. Both share one clock that ticks once per operation, so the one doing extra work visibly falls behind.

## Common mistakes

- **Counting past n.** Each level's range is capped: min(n, hi) − lo + 1.
- **Forgetting that cur itself counts.** Stepping inside uses one of the k steps (cur is the first number in its subtree).
- **Using the walk for huge n.** A billion steps is too slow; skipping is what makes it fast.
