## The problem

You get a grid with `m` rows and `n` columns. Return all its values in **spiral order**: start at the top left, go
right along the top row, down the right-hand column, left along the bottom row and up the left-hand column, then
carry on the same way around the next ring inwards until every value is listed once.

For example, the grid with rows `8 1 6 3`, `4 9 2 7` and `5 0 6 1` gives `8, 1, 6, 3, 7, 1, 6, 0, 5, 4, 9, 2`.

## The key question

> **How do I know when to turn, and which cells are still left to read?**

The grid doesn't have to be square, so the inner rings can end as a single row or a single column. Both approaches
must avoid reading a cell twice. See [Grids](/concepts/grids/) for `grid[r][c]` and moving in directions.

## Approach 1: Walk and turn, marking visited cells

Move one cell at a time, starting heading right. Before each move, look at the next cell: if it's off the grid or
already visited, turn right (right, then down, then left, then up, then right again). A second grid of true/false
values, `seen`, remembers which cells were visited.

<Viz client:visible approach="seen" />

<Complexity time="O(m · n)" space="O(m · n)">
**Time.** Each of the `m · n` cells is visited once, with a constant amount of checking: **O(m · n)**.

**Space.** The `seen` grid has a true/false value for every cell: **O(m · n)**, on top of the answer.

**What it wastes:** a whole grid of marks. The cells not yet read always form a rectangle, so four numbers describe
them.
</Complexity>

## Approach 2: Shrinking borders (optimal)

Keep `top`, `bottom`, `left` and `right`: the edges of the rectangle not yet read. Read its top row and move `top`
down; read its right column and move `right` left; then its bottom row, and its left column. Repeat while
`top ≤ bottom` and `left ≤ right`.

Two checks in the middle matter. After the top row and the right column, the rectangle may have no rows left
(`top > bottom`), or no columns left (`left > right`). Without the checks, a single leftover row or column would be
read twice, once in each direction.

<Viz client:visible approach="borders" />

<Complexity time="O(m · n)" space="O(1)">
**Time.** Every cell is read exactly once: **O(m · n)**.

**Space.** Four numbers besides the answer: **O(1)** extra space.
</Complexity>

## Comparing the approaches

Both on the same grid:

<Compare client:visible />

| Approach | Time | Extra space | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Walk and turn, marking visited | O(m · n) | O(m · n) | a true/false mark per cell |
| Shrinking borders | O(m · n) | O(1) | four numbers |

Both read each cell once, so there's no growth chart; the difference is the memory.

## Common mistakes

- **Dropping the checks before the bottom row and the left column.** A grid like `1 2 3` (one row) would come out as
  `1, 2, 3, 2, 1`.
- **Mixing up rows and columns.** The right column is `grid[r][right]`, with `r` changing; the bottom row is
  `grid[bottom][c]`, with `c` changing.
- **Assuming the grid is square.** Test with 1 × n, m × 1 and 2 × 3 grids, where the rings end differently.
