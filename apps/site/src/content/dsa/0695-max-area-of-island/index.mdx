## The problem

You get a grid of 1s (land) and 0s (water). An island is a group of land cells joined horizontally or vertically. Return
the number of cells in the largest island, or 0 if there's no land.

For example, in the grid with rows `110010`, `100011`, `001011` and `111000`, the islands have 3, 5 and 4 cells, so the
answer is 5.

## The key question

> **How do I measure every island without measuring any of them twice?**

See [Searching grids](/concepts/searching-grids/): flood fill. This is the measuring version of [Number of
Islands](/dsa/number-of-islands/).

## Approach 1: Measure the island of every land cell

For every land cell, run a fresh flood fill from it and record the size of its island.

<Viz client:visible approach="everyCell" />

<Complexity time="O((m · n)²)" space="O(m · n)">
**Time.** Each land cell re-measures its whole island. If one island covers the grid, that's `m · n` flood fills of
`m · n` cells each: **O((m · n)²)**.

**Space.** One flood fill's seen set and stack: **O(m · n)**.

**What it wastes:** every cell of an island measures the same island again.
</Complexity>

## Approach 2: Measure each island once (optimal)

Keep a single seen set for the whole grid. Scan the cells; when a land cell isn't seen yet, flood-fill its island,
marking every cell seen and counting them, and keep the biggest count.

<Viz client:visible approach="once" />

<Complexity time="O(m · n)" space="O(m · n)">
**Time.** Every cell is visited once in the scan and at most once in a flood fill: **O(m · n)**.

**Space.** The seen set and the stack: **O(m · n)**. Overwriting land with water as you visit it saves the set, if
changing the grid is allowed.
</Complexity>

## Common mistakes

- **Counting diagonal neighbours.** Islands join only up, down, left and right here.
- **Marking cells seen when they're visited instead of when they're pushed.** A cell can then be pushed twice and
  counted twice.
- **Deep recursion.** A recursive flood fill on a large island can overflow the call stack; an explicit stack can't.
