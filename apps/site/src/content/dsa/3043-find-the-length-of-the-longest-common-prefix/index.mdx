## The problem

You get two lists of positive whole numbers, `arr1` and `arr2`. For a number x from arr1 and y from arr2, their common prefix is the run of leading digits they share (for 1203 and 120, it's 120: three digits). Return the length of the longest common prefix over all such pairs, or 0 if no pair shares even a first digit.

For `arr1 = 125, 1203, 98` and `arr2 = 120, 9, 1250`, the answer is 3 (125 and 1250, or 1203 and 120).

## The key question

> **How can one number be compared with a whole list at once?**

Put the list in a trie of digits. Every prefix any of its numbers has is a path from the root, so a number shares k digits with some number in the list exactly when it can walk k steps down.

## Approach 1: Compare every pair of numbers

For every x in arr1 and y in arr2, count the leading digits they share, and keep the largest count.

<Viz client:visible approach="pairs" />

<Complexity time="O(n · m · D)" space="O(D)">
**Time.** n · m pairs, each compared over up to D digits: **O(n · m · D)**.

**Space.** The digits being compared: **O(D)**.

**What it wastes:** re-reading the same prefixes for every pair.
</Complexity>

## Approach 2: A trie of arr1's digits, walked by arr2 (optimal)

Insert every number of arr1 into a trie, one digit per node. For each number of arr2, walk down while the trie has its next digit; the number of steps is its best shared prefix. Keep the largest.

<Viz client:visible approach="trie" />

<Complexity time="O((n + m) · D)" space="O(n · D)">
**Time.** Each number is inserted or walked once, one step per digit: **O((n + m) · D)**.

**Space.** The trie: up to **n · D** nodes.
</Complexity>

## Comparing the approaches

Race two approaches on the same input. Both share one clock that ticks once per operation, so the one doing extra work visibly falls behind.

| Approach | Time | Space | Idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compare every pair of numbers | O(n · m · D) | O(D) | Every pair |
| A trie of arr1's digits, walked by arr2 | **O((n + m) · D)** | O(n · D) | Compare with everything at once |

## Common mistakes

- **Comparing the numbers' values instead of their digits.** 120 and 1203 share "120", though one is ten times the other.
- **Building the trie from both lists.** Only one list goes in; the other walks it.
- **Stopping at the first full match.** Keep the longest over all of arr2.
