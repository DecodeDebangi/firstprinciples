## The problem

You get a sentence of words separated by single spaces, and a `searchWord`. Return the position (counting from 1) of the first word that starts with `searchWord`, or −1 if there is none.

For the sentence `the cat sat on a catalog` and the search word `cat`, the answer is 2.

## The key question

> **Do you need the words as a separate list?**

No. Reading the sentence letter by letter, a space ends a word, and a running count of matched letters says whether the current word still starts with the search word.

## Approach 1: Split into words, check each

Split the sentence at the spaces, then check each word's start against the search word, returning the first match's position.

<Viz client:visible approach="split" />

<Complexity time="O(length of sentence)" space="O(length of sentence)">
**Time.** Splitting and checking read each letter a fixed number of times: **O(length)**.

**Space.** The list of words: **O(length)**.

**What it wastes:** memory for a list of words that's read once.
</Complexity>

## Approach 2: One pass over the letters, no splitting (optimal)

Walk the letters with a word counter and a count of letters matched. A space starts the next word; a matching letter extends the match; a mismatch marks the word as hopeless until the next space.

<Viz client:visible approach="scan" />

<Complexity time="O(length of sentence)" space="O(1)">
**Time.** One pass over the sentence: **O(length)**.

**Space.** Two counters: **O(1)**.
</Complexity>

## Comparing the approaches

Race two approaches on the same input. Both share one clock that ticks once per operation, so the one doing extra work visibly falls behind.

<Compare client:visible />

| Approach | Time | Space | Idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| Split into words, check each | O(length of sentence) | O(length of sentence) | Split, then check |
| One pass over the letters, no splitting | **O(length of sentence)** | O(1) | Count while reading |

Both approaches read the sentence once, so there's no growth chart. The difference is the list of words the first one builds.

## Common mistakes

- **Returning a position counted from 0.** The first word is position 1.
- **Finding the search word in the middle of a word.** It must be at a word's start.
- **Returning the last match.** The first matching word wins.
