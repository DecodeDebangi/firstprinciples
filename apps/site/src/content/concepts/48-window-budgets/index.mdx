[Sliding window](/concepts/sliding-window/) introduced windows that add one item on the right and drop items on the
left. Variable-window questions come in two flavours, and the difference is **when you shrink**.

## The longest window within a budget

"Longest stretch with at most `k` zeros", "longest with at most two different letters", "longest without repeats": a
window is allowed as long as it stays within some budget. Grow it to the right every step. Only when it goes **over**
budget, shrink from the left until it's back within; then the window is a candidate, so compare its length with the
best.

<Viz client:visible approach="budget" />

That's [Max Consecutive Ones III](/dsa/max-consecutive-ones-iii/) (the zeros are the ones you're allowed to flip) and
[Longest Substring Without Repeating Characters](/dsa/longest-substring-without-repeating-characters/).

## The shortest window that's enough

"Shortest stretch adding up to at least `target`": here a window is a candidate once it **meets** the target. So after
adding each value, **while** the window still meets the target, record its length and drop its leftmost value to see if
something shorter works too.

<Viz client:visible approach="shortest" />

That's [Minimum Size Subarray Sum](/dsa/minimum-size-subarray-sum/). It relies on the values being positive: adding a
value can only raise the sum and dropping one can only lower it. With negatives, use [prefix
sums](/concepts/prefix-sums/) instead.

## The budget in disguise: replacements

"Longest stretch you can make all one letter by changing at most `k` letters": the cheapest way to make a window all one
letter is to keep its most common letter and change the rest. So the window's cost is
`length − count of its most common letter`, and it's within budget while that's at most `k`. Keep a count per letter,
and it's the longest-within-budget window again. That's [Longest Repeating Character
Replacement](/dsa/longest-repeating-character-replacement/).

## The fixed window with counts

When the window's length is fixed, like "does some rearrangement of `p` appear in `s`?", slide a window of
`length(p)` and keep its letter counts, updating two of them per step. That's [Permutation in
String](/dsa/permutation-in-string/), a yes/no version of [Find All Anagrams in a
String](/dsa/find-all-anagrams-in-a-string/). The plainest fixed window of all, a running sum, is [Maximum Average
Subarray I](/dsa/maximum-average-subarray-i/).

## Interview theory

<QA q="How do the longest-window and shortest-window templates differ?">
Longest: grow every step, shrink only while the window breaks the rule, then record. Shortest: grow every step, and while
the window meets the target, record and shrink. Both are O(n) because each pointer only moves forward.
</QA>

<QA q="How do you decide whether a window of letters can be made all one letter with k changes?">
Its length minus the count of its most common letter must be at most k: keep the most common letter, change the rest.
</QA>

<QA q="When doesn't a sliding window work for sums?">
When values can be negative: a longer window can have a smaller sum, so shrinking or growing no longer moves the sum in
a predictable direction. Prefix sums with a hash map handle that case.
</QA>
