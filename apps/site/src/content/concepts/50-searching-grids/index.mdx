[Graphs](/concepts/graphs/) introduced breadth-first and depth-first search. Many interview graph problems never
mention a graph: they hand you a grid. Each cell is a node, and it's joined to its neighbours, usually the four up, down,
left and right (see [Grids](/concepts/grids/) for neighbours and bounds). No adjacency list needs building: the
neighbours are worked out on the spot.

## Many starts at once (multi-source BFS)

"Distance from every cell to the nearest exit", "minutes until every orange rots": the spread begins from several cells
at once. Don't run one search per start. Put **all** the starts in the queue first, at distance 0, and run one BFS.
It grows from all of them together, ring by ring, so each cell is first reached from its nearest start.

<Viz client:visible approach="layers" />

One BFS: **O(rows · columns)**, however many starts. That's [Rotting Oranges](/dsa/rotting-oranges/). With one start
and diagonal moves allowed (8 neighbours), the same BFS gives [Shortest Path in Binary
Matrix](/dsa/shortest-path-in-binary-matrix/).

## Measuring a region (flood fill)

To count the cells of an island, start anywhere on it and visit every land cell joined to it, with a stack (DFS) or a
queue (BFS); both visit the same cells. Mark a cell as seen when you push it, so that two neighbours can't both add it.

<Viz client:visible approach="area" />

Run this from every unvisited land cell and keep the biggest: [Max Area of Island](/dsa/max-area-of-island/). It's the
counting version of [Number of Islands](/dsa/number-of-islands/). The same "visit everything reachable" search works on
any graph, such as rooms with keys to other rooms: [Keys and Rooms](/dsa/keys-and-rooms/).

## Searching backwards from the targets

"Which cells can reach the ocean?" Searching from every cell separately repeats work, since many paths overlap. Turn it
round: start from the targets (the ocean's edge) and walk each move **backwards**. Water flows from high to low, so
backwards means moving to cells at least as high. One search from the targets finds every cell that can reach them.
[Pacific Atlantic Water Flow](/dsa/pacific-atlantic-water-flow/) does this for two oceans and keeps the cells both
searches reach.

## Interview theory

<QA q="How do you find each cell's distance to the nearest of several sources?">
Multi-source BFS: put every source in the queue at distance 0, then run an ordinary BFS. Each cell's first visit comes
from its nearest source. O(rows · columns).
</QA>

<QA q="When should you search from the targets instead of from every start?">
When many starts share the same few targets. Walking each rule backwards from the targets visits every cell once,
instead of once per start.
</QA>

<QA q="BFS or DFS for flood fill?">
Either: both visit exactly the cells joined to the start. BFS is needed when the order matters, such as shortest
distances; for areas and counts, use whichever you like, preferring an explicit stack to avoid deep recursion on big
grids.
</QA>
